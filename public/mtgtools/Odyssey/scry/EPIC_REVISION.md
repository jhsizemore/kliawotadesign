# Scry: epic introduction and working-rule showcase

This is a website implementation, not a generated screenshot. The generated concept paintings, pottery fragment and invented Charles Jalabert attribution were not used.

## What changed

- Restored a small tilt on the native full-art **Odysseus, Cunning Voyager**. A single parent transform is applied after the actual artwork decodes; no compounded drop-shadow filters. The existing source-derived renderer and published placement remain authoritative.
- Shortened **The Odyssey in Magic** into an introduction to the return from Troy and the people waiting in Ithaca, with an actual ancient manuscript fragment and the same ship symbol printed on the cards.
- The ship is described as a stylised ancient oared vessel used as the voyage's emblem. It is not presented as an archaeological reconstruction or an excavated flagship of a historical Odysseus.
- Every highlighted feature has a **working rule, working implementation or thematic-package explanation**, followed by an explicit development qualifier and a link to an example card. These paragraphs are explanations, not purported quotations from the canonical card file.
- Manifest Fate currently means: look at the top two cards of your library, manifest one face down as a 2/2 creature, then exile the other. This states the requested working interpretation without changing any card text.
- Eight reviewed landscapes, seascapes and architectural landscapes replace figure-led or crowded battle backdrops. Each has source attribution and a note distinguishing atmosphere from actual Homeric geography.
- The temporary spoiler contains **Autolycus showing his wares**, inset on a warm, subtly textured CSS background. The complete source image is contained, not cropped into a background. The candidate's existing artwork assignment is not changed.

## The genuine Odyssey artifact

**Papyrus fragment with lines from Homer's Odyssey**, Greek, Ptolemaic, ca. 285–250 BCE. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, 09.182.50. Gift of Egypt Exploration Fund, 1909. The museum identifies it as a fragment of Book 20, including variant lines not preserved in the standard modern text.

Collection record: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/248134
Image source: https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/api/collection/v1/iiif/248134/534364/main-image
Rights: Public Domain / The Met Open Access.

The small introduction uses a crop removing the photograph's empty grey margins. The complete photograph and source metadata remain available in the image viewer. No text or object surface has been invented or reconstructed.

## Autolycus, correctly identified

**Autolycus**, Charles Robert Leslie, ca. 1836. Oil on canvas. Victoria and Albert Museum, FA.115[O]. The painting depicts Shakespeare's pedlar in *The Winter's Tale*, not Homer's Autolycus. The caption makes this namesake connection explicit.

Museum record: https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O80881/autolycus-oil-painting-leslie-charles-robert/
Reproduction: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Leslie_-_Autolycus.jpg
Original reproduction SHA-1: a1e6d469a5cc21b8fff5dbaf84781eb7998f4f2f.

This reproduction is 705 × 537 pixels, displayed as a contained inset rather than upscaled into a full-bleed image. The original photograph was checked against the published checksum. No generative enhancement was used.
